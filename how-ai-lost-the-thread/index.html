Transformers — The Problem
Why Earlier Networks Struggled with Language
Language is a sequence, and the words that belong together can be far apart.

Convolutional nets
RNNs & LSTMs

Good at: simple patterns in a fixed set of inputs.
Good at: local patterns — a few neighbouring words.
Good at: reading in order, carrying a memory along.
Struggles with: order and context. No memory of what came before, and not enough depth.
Struggles with: words that belong together but are far apart.
Struggles with: long texts, because the memory fades. And speed: word 100 waits for word 99.
Transformers — The Idea
Every Word Looks at Every Other Word
Self-attention connects any two words directly, however far apart they are.
Before: word by word
An RNN passes its memory along: 7 hops from “trophy” to “it”, fading on the way.
Transformer: one step
Every pair is connected directly, and all words are processed at once, so training runs in parallel.
attention weights illustrative
Transformers — Inside
Inside a Transformer
A stack of identical blocks: attention first, then a small classic network.

Self-attention

Each word asks every other word “how relevant are you to me?”, then mixes in their meaning, weighted by the answer.

Multi-head attention

Several attentions run side by side. One head may track grammar, another what “it” refers to.

Positional encoding

Attention sees all words at once, so order is lost. A position signal keeps “dog bites man” ≠ “man bites dog”.

Feed-forward, normalisation & shortcuts

A small classic network per word — perceptrons again. Shortcuts and normalisation keep a deep stack stable to train.

No self-attention, no parallel training — no GPT-4.
